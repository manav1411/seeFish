# SeeFish population model coverage

The browser model is built from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) 2021 Census General Community Profile DataPack for Greater Capital City Statistical Areas. The source is the official `2021_GCP_GCCSA_for_AUS_short-header.zip`, retrieved 19 September 2026 from the [ABS Census DataPacks page](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/datapacks). ABS publishes the DataPack under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 Australia.

| Feature | Table and universe | What the model does | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Age, sex and personal income | G17, people aged 15+, weekly total personal income by age band and sex | Uses the observed joint cells for Australia and all eight Greater Capital City Statistical Areas. G04 single-year counts divide each G17 age band into single ages. Income bands are converted to annual values by multiplying by 52. | Census records sex, while the UI asks who the user wants to meet. Single-age allocation assumes the income mix is stable inside each published age band. Counts are Census-night usual residents, not current population projections. |
| Exact age | G04, age by sex | Preserves each city's observed single-year age shape from 18 through 80. The centre estimate allocates each G17 income cell using that shape. | The displayed range also tries the lowest and highest allocations possible from the published age-band and single-age margins. If an income range cuts a band, that band's bounded contribution can range from none to all. |
| Cultural background / ancestry | G08, selected ancestry by parents' birthplace | Uses observed city ancestry response margins. Multiple selections use independence only as a centre scenario. The range uses the Fréchet bounds for the union, then the exact possible intersection bounds between that union and the selected demographic cohort. | Ancestry allows up to two responses, so categories overlap. G08 does not cross ancestry with age, sex or income; the resulting mathematical range may be very wide. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander ancestry is not the same as Indigenous status. |
| Height | ABS National Health Survey 2022 Table 8.1 and methodology | Uses the published male means 177.4, 176.4, 175.6, 175.6, 173.6, 172.3 and 170.2 cm and female means 162.9, 163.4, 162.9, 162.5, 160.7, 158.7 and 157.1 cm for ages 18–24 through 75+. | ABS does not publish a full height distribution in this table. Normal standard deviations are explicit sensitivity assumptions: 6.0, 7.2 and 8.5 cm for men; 5.5, 6.7 and 8.0 cm for women. No city or ancestry adjustment is made. In 2022, 41.8% of respondents aged 15+ had height, weight, or both imputed. |

The compact artifact is generated by `scripts/build-abs-model.mjs`; it contains about 18,000 weighted cells and ancestry margins. Run `sh scripts/rebuild-abs-model.sh` to download both official snapshots, verify their reviewed SHA-256 hashes, check the published height values, and reproduce it. The checked-in artifact makes results deterministic and avoids relying on ABS API availability at runtime.

Counts are perturbed by the ABS to protect confidentiality. The displayed range is a sensitivity range, not a confidence interval. It is built from possible within-age-band allocations, the disclosed height-spread variants, and—when selected—mathematical ancestry intersection bounds. It is capped by both the relevant demographic cohort and the model denominator. Unknown income is retained when no income range is selected and excluded when a range is applied. Income preferences are two-ended ranges; the $182,000 upper bound includes the open-ended ABS band beginning at $182,000, without extrapolating a tail above that threshold.
